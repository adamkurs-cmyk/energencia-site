01 — Learn by doing

Learn by doing.

Most of the weekend is spent playing.

You'll learn how to hold the bowls, build a session, work with rhythm, silence and pacing, and guide people through a complete sound bath. You'll practise in pairs, in small groups and on your own, receiving as much as you facilitate.

The aim isn't to teach you every possible technique. It's to help you leave feeling ready to lead your own sessions.

02 — Who it's for

This training is for you if you:

  • 01teach yoga, meditation or breathwork and want to bring sound into your classes
  • 02work with the body and would like to expand your practice
  • 03have been playing bowls on your own and want more structure
  • 04are completely new but feel drawn to working with sound

You don't need your own bowls and you don't need any previous experience.

03 — What you'll learn

By the end of the weekend you'll know how to:

  • 01choose and care for Tibetan singing bowls
  • 02strike and play them with confidence
  • 03create grounding and more activating parts of a session
  • 04use rhythm, repetition and silence intentionally
  • 05guide people into and out of relaxation
  • 06hold space for a group with confidence
  • 07facilitate a complete sound bath from beginning to end

You'll also understand the foundations behind the practice, including nervous system regulation, brain waves, the role of rhythm, and where sound healing fits within modern wellbeing.

04 — How it works

One online theory evening on Friday, 19:30 to 21:00, then two full days in person at De Moon Yoga in València, both from 10:00, around eight hours each with a lunch break. Kept to a small group so everyone gets hands-on time and honest feedback. Taught in English. Bowls are provided, small and large sets, so you don't need your own to begin. You finish by facilitating your own full sound bath, assessed on day two.

FridayOnline · 19:30–21:00

The theory evening. We cover the foundations online so both days in the room can be spent playing and practising.

Day oneDe Moon Yoga, València · from 10:00 · ~8 hours

  • Opening sound bath, then an opening circle and introductions
  • Handling the bowls: what to avoid, and how to hold them properly
  • Striking the bowl: mallet, space and tempo, then freestyle practice feeling the sound on small and large sets
  • Working the rim for a more activating sound, then freestyle rim practice on small and large sets
  • Lunch
  • In two rotating groups, one playing and one receiving: how to open, how to build up with rhythm and repetition, grounding versus activating sound, and how to close
  • How to open and close the space
  • Two hours of solo practice, a full sound bath each, leaning more activating or more grounding

Day twoFrom 10:00 · ~8 hours

  • Opening sound bath and a sharing circle
  • Revision of day one
  • Guiding, rituals, the waking-up ritual and chimes
  • Two hours of guided practice with cues
  • The exam: each person facilitates a full sound bath of around twenty minutes, with timing scaled to the size of the group

05 — Why I created this training

Why I created this training

I wanted to teach for a long time before I finally felt ready.

When I looked at other trainings, many felt overwhelming. Some tried to cover every instrument. Others stayed so broad that people finished without knowing how to actually begin.

I wanted something different.

A small training focused on one instrument, plenty of practice, honest feedback and enough theory to understand why you're doing what you're doing.

My goal is simple.

By the end of the weekend, you should feel confident enough to go home and facilitate your own sound bath.

10 — Questions

FAQ

Do I need any experience?

No. Complete beginners are welcome, and so are teachers and bodyworkers who want to go deeper. What matters is that you want to practice.

Do I need my own bowls?

Not to start. Bowls are provided for the training, small and large sets. I'll also show you how to choose your own when you're ready to buy.

Is it certified?

You get a certificate of completion from me. This is my own independent training, so it's the real, practical skill of facilitating a sound bath rather than an outside accreditation, and I'm clear about that.
